Production & profit

We switch sides of the market. A firm turns inputs into output and picks the quantity that earns the most profit. First the profit problem, then what is inside the firm: production functions, isoquants and returns to scale.

Profit maximization

Let \(q\ge0\) be the firm's output. Profit is revenue minus cost:

\[ \pi(q)=R(q)-C(q). \]

If the firm produces \(q>0\), it chooses the quantity where marginal revenue equals marginal cost:

\[ MR=\frac{dR}{dq}=\frac{dC}{dq}=MC. \]

A price taker faces a fixed market price \(p\), so \(R(q)=pq\) and \(MR=p\). The condition becomes \(p=MC(q)\).

Common trap

\(p=MC\) is required only at an interior optimum \(q^*>0\). If \(p<MC(0)\), solving \(p=MC\) gives a negative \(q\), which is infeasible. Every unit costs more than it earns, so the firm produces \(q^*=0\) (a corner solution).

A price-taking firm cost \(c(q)=q^2+kq\), so \(MC=2q+k\)

Things to try

  • Slide the price below \(k\). What happens to \(q^*\) and to profit?
  • Raise \(k\) with the price fixed. Why does the firm produce less?
  • Look at the shaded gap between the price line and the \(MC\) curve. Why is it the profit when there is no fixed cost?

Production functions

A production function \(F:\mathbb R_+^2\to\mathbb R_+\) gives the output made from capital \(K\) and labor \(L\). For constants \(A,\alpha,\beta>0\), the three standard forms are:

Name\(F(K,L)\)Inputs are…
Cobb-Douglas\(AK^\alpha L^\beta\)imperfectly substitutable
Perfect substitutes\(\alpha K+\beta L\)interchangeable at a fixed rate
Perfect complements\(\min\{\alpha K,\beta L\}\)used in a fixed ratio
Different from utility

Utility is ordinal: only the ranking matters, so any increasing transformation is the same preference. A production function is cardinal: the output level itself matters, so transforming \(F\) changes the firm's technology.

Isoquants, marginal products and the MRTS

An isoquant collects all input combinations \((K,L)\) that produce the same output, just as an indifference curve collects bundles with the same utility. The marginal products are

\[ MP_L=\frac{\partial F}{\partial L},\qquad MP_K=\frac{\partial F}{\partial K}, \]

and the isoquant's slope is the marginal rate of technical substitution:

\[ MRTS_{KL}=-\frac{MP_K}{MP_L}. \]

It is the amount of labor the firm can give up for one more unit of capital while keeping output constant.

  • Perfect substitutes: does the MRTS change along the isoquant? How is it related to \(\alpha\) and \(\beta\)?
  • Perfect complements: can you find an MRTS at the corner? (It is undefined.)

Returns to scale

What happens to output when both inputs are multiplied by \(x>1\)?

  • Constant (CRS): \(F(xK,xL)=xF(K,L)\). Doubling inputs doubles output.
  • Decreasing (DRS): \(F(xK,xL)<xF(K,L)\).
  • Increasing (IRS): \(F(xK,xL)>xF(K,L)\).

For Cobb-Douglas, \((xK)^\alpha(xL)^\beta=x^{\alpha+\beta}F(K,L)\), so the answer is read off the exponents: \(\alpha+\beta=1\) is CRS, \(<1\) is DRS, \(>1\) is IRS. Perfect substitutes and perfect complements are always CRS.

Common trap

Returns to scale are about scaling both inputs together. Diminishing marginal product is about raising one input with the other fixed. A function can have IRS and still have diminishing \(MP_K\).

Worked exercises

Profit maximization

Firm A is a price taker with cost function \(c(q)=q^2+4q\). (a) Find \(MC\) and \(MR\) when \(p=10\). (b) Find the profit-maximizing quantity and the profit. (c) What is the profit-maximizing quantity if the price falls to \(p=3\)?

Show solution

(a) \(MC=\frac{dc}{dq}=2q+4\). For a price taker \(MR=p=10\).

(b) \(10=2q+4\Rightarrow q^*=3\). Profit: \(\pi=10(3)-(9+12)=9\). \(MC\) is increasing, so this is a maximum.

(c) \(3=2q+4\) gives \(q=-\tfrac12\), which is infeasible. Since \(MC(0)=4>3=p\), profit \(\pi(q)=-q-q^2\) falls as \(q\) rises, so \(q^*=0\) and \(\pi=0\).

MRTS of a Cobb-Douglas firm

Firm B has \(F(K,L)=K^{1/3}L^{1/2}\). Find \(MP_L\), \(MP_K\) and \(MRTS_{KL}\), and evaluate the MRTS at \((K,L)=(4,6)\).

Show solution

\(MP_L=\tfrac12K^{1/3}L^{-1/2}\) and \(MP_K=\tfrac13K^{-2/3}L^{1/2}\).

\(MRTS_{KL}=-\dfrac{\tfrac13K^{-2/3}L^{1/2}}{\tfrac12K^{1/3}L^{-1/2}}=-\dfrac23\cdot\dfrac LK\).

At \((4,6)\): \(-\tfrac23\cdot\tfrac64=-1\). The firm can give up one unit of labor for one more unit of capital at constant output.

Returns to scale

Classify (a) \(F(K,L)=K^{3/4}L^{1/2}\) and (b) \(F(K,L)=\min\{2K,L\}\).

Show solution

(a) \(F(xK,xL)=x^{3/4+1/2}K^{3/4}L^{1/2}=x^{5/4}F(K,L)>xF(K,L)\): increasing returns (exponents sum to \(5/4>1\)).

(b) \(\min\{2xK,xL\}=x\min\{2K,L\}=xF(K,L)\): constant returns.

A piecewise production function

Firm C uses only capital, with \(F(K)=\min\{3,\ \sqrt K+1\}\). Find \(MP_K\).

Show solution

The pieces meet where \(\sqrt K+1=3\), i.e. \(K=4\). So \(F=\sqrt K+1\) for \(K<4\) and \(F=3\) for \(K\ge4\).

\(MP_K=\dfrac{1}{2\sqrt K}\) for \(K<4\) and \(MP_K=0\) for \(K>4\). At \(K=4\) the left derivative is \(1/4\) and the right derivative is \(0\), so \(MP_K\) is undefined at the kink. Capital has diminishing marginal product, and beyond \(K=4\) it adds no output at all.

Isoquants of perfect substitutes

Firm D has \(F(K,L)=K+2L\). Describe the isoquants for \(q=4\) and \(q=8\) and find the MRTS.

Show solution

Solve \(K+2L=q\) for \(L=\frac{q-K}{2}\). For \(q=4\) the intercepts are \((K,L)=(4,0)\) and \((0,2)\); for \(q=8\) they are \((8,0)\) and \((0,4)\).

Both are straight lines with slope \(-1/2\); the one farther from the origin gives more output. Also \(MP_K=1\), \(MP_L=2\), so \(MRTS_{KL}=-\tfrac12\) everywhere: one unit of \(L\) replaces two units of \(K\).
