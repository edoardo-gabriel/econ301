Utility & indifference curves

A utility function is a convenient way of writing down preferences: bigger number, better bundle.

Utility functions represent preferences

\(u(a,b)\) represents preferences if \(u(X)\ge u(Y)\) exactly when \(X\succeq Y\). We will use three families, for \(\alpha,\beta>0\):

  • Perfect substitutes: \(u=\alpha a+\beta b\). Straight indifference curves.
  • Perfect complements: \(u=\min\{\alpha a,\beta b\}\). L-shaped curves, kink where \(\alpha a=\beta b\).
  • Cobb-Douglas: \(u=a^\alpha b^\beta\). Smooth, convex curves.

Indifference curves and the MRS

An indifference curve fixes a level of utility: all \((a,b)\) with \(u(a,b)=\bar u\). Its slope is the marginal rate of substitution,

\[ \mathrm{MRS}_{ab}=-\frac{MU_a}{MU_b}, \qquad MU_a=\frac{\partial u}{\partial a},\ MU_b=\frac{\partial u}{\partial b}. \]

\(|\mathrm{MRS}_{ab}|\) is the individual's relative value of the goods: how many units of \(b\) she would give up for one more unit of \(a\). Compare this with the market's relative value, \(p_a/p_b\). That comparison is what drives optimal choice on the next page.

Things to try

  • Cobb-Douglas: move right along one curve. Does \(|\mathrm{MRS}|\) rise or fall? (Falls: convexity.)
  • Increase \(\alpha\). What happens to the curve's steepness? Why does a higher \(\alpha\) mean you value \(a\) more?
  • Perfect substitutes: is the MRS the same everywhere? Perfect complements: what is it at the kink?

Utility is ordinal

Only the ranking matters, not the size of the numbers. If \(f\) is a positive monotonic (strictly increasing) transformation, \(f(u)\) represents the same preferences as \(u\): same indifference curves, same MRS, same optimal choices. Marginal utilities do change (they get scaled by \(f'\)), but their ratio does not.

Same preferences or not? transform \(u=ab\) and compare rankings

Common trap

A utility difference of 3 does not mean "worth $3 more" or "three times as happy". Utility numbers have no units.

Worked exercises

Handout 2, Question 1

Consumer A's utility function is \(u(a,b)=\sqrt a+\sqrt b\). Graph the indifference curves corresponding to \(u(a,b)=1\) and \(u(a,b)=2\).

Show solution

Fix \(u=\bar u\): \(\sqrt a+\sqrt b=\bar u\Rightarrow b=(\bar u-\sqrt a)^2\), valid for \(0\le a\le\bar u^2\).

  • \(\bar u=1\): \(b=(1-\sqrt a)^2\) from \((0,1)\) to \((1,0)\).
  • \(\bar u=2\): \(b=(2-\sqrt a)^2\) from \((0,4)\) to \((4,0)\).

Both curves are downward sloping (more is better) and bowed toward the origin (convex), and the higher curve is farther from the origin. Check a point: \((1,1)\) gives \(1+1=2\), so it is on the \(u=2\) curve.

Computing marginal utility and MRS

For \(u(a,b)=3a^2b+ab^2\), find \(MU_a\), \(MU_b\) and \(\mathrm{MRS}_{ab}\) at \((a,b)=(2,3)\).

Show solution

\(MU_a=\partial u/\partial a=6ab+b^2=36+9=45\).
\(MU_b=\partial u/\partial b=3a^2+2ab=12+12=24\).
\(\mathrm{MRS}_{ab}=-45/24=-15/8\). At this bundle she would give up \(15/8\) units of \(b\) for one more unit of \(a\).
