Demand curves

What happens to the optimal bundle when a price changes? Trace it out and you have a demand curve.

From optimal choice to demand

The demand curve for good \(a\), \(D_a(p_a)\), is the optimal quantity of \(a\) as a function of its own price, holding preferences, income \(Y\) and the other price \(p_b\) fixed. To get it: solve the optimal-choice problem for general \(p_a\).

Scope

For Midterm 1 you need to derive demand curves and add them. Elasticities, normal/inferior goods, substitutes/complements classification, and income and substitution effects are not on the exam.

Market demand

Market demand is the sum of everyone's demand at each price: add quantities, not prices. Graphically that is a horizontal sum. If one consumer stops buying above some price, the market curve has a kink there.

Worked exercise

Market demand with a jump

Consumer 1 has \(u_1=ab^2\) and income \(Y_1=60\). Consumer 2 has \(u_2=2a+b\) and income \(Y_2=30\). Both face \(p_b=3\). Find each consumer's demand for \(a\) and the market demand for all \(p_a>0\).

Show solution

Consumer 1 (Cobb-Douglas): the share spent on \(a\) is \(\frac{1}{1+2}=\frac13\), so \(D_1(p_a)=\dfrac{60/3}{p_a}=\dfrac{20}{p_a}\).

Consumer 2 (perfect substitutes): compare utility per dollar, \(2/p_a\) versus \(1/3\). She buys \(a\) only if \(2/p_a>1/3\), i.e. \(p_a<6\):

\[ D_2(p_a)=\begin{cases}30/p_a & p_a<6\ [0,\,5] & p_a=6\ 0 & p_a>6.\end{cases} \]

Market: add at each price.

\[ D(p_a)=\begin{cases}50/p_a & p_a<6\ 20/6+[0,5]=[3.33,\,8.33] & p_a=6\ 20/p_a & p_a>6.\end{cases} \]

The market curve drops from \(50/6\approx8.33\) to \(20/6\approx3.33\) at \(p_a=6\) when consumer 2 leaves the market, and there is a flat piece at \(p_a=6\) in between. Try recreating it in the builder: consumer 1 "Cobb-Douglas, a third on a" with income 60 and consumer 2 "Perfect substitutes" with income 30 (set \(p_b=3\) in the builder; the substitutes preset is \(2a+b\)).
